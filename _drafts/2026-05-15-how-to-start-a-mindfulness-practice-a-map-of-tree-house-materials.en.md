If you want to start a mindfulness practice with **Tree House**, the simplest answer is this:

- begin with the **blog** if you want a short explanation or a gentle way in,
- begin with **Urban Forest Bathing** if you want to work with a weekly rhythm right away,
- begin with the **Page-a-Day calendar** if you want a daily point of return,
- begin with **Sky Today** if you want the lightest possible pause.

This guide brings those four paths together and shows how they form one system. Nature is an important support and setting for the practice here, but the point of entry can be different for each reader.

## Mindfulness can begin with one quiet step

It helps to choose one entry point that fits what you need right now.

If you want to understand the theme first, begin with [Mindfulness in Nature: Where to Begin If You Don’t Meditate](/en/mindfulness-in-nature-where-to-begin-if-you-dont-meditate/). If you want practical suggestions right away, open [5 Simple Mindfulness Exercises Inspired by Nature](/en/5-simple-mindfulness-exercises-inspired-by-nature/).

If a ready-made rhythm helps more than reading, go straight to [Urban Forest Bathing](/en/materials/urban-bathing/).

## How the whole system fits together

The simplest way to see it is like this:

1. The **blog** helps you find language, questions, and a first direction.
2. **Urban Forest Bathing** turns that direction into weekly practice.
3. The **Page-a-Day calendar** shows the current day and the current weekly card.
4. **Sky Today** brings you back to light, sky, and the rhythm of the day.
5. The **52-week structure** shows the wider shape of the whole cycle if you want to see how it all connects.

Each of these pages supports a different level of contact with mindfulness: introductory, weekly, daily, or observational.

## Urban Forest Bathing and the 52-week structure

[Urban Forest Bathing](/en/materials/urban-bathing/) is the main practice path on the site. It is a series of weekly cards inspired by nature, mindfulness, and the rhythm of the seasons. Each card offers one short practice or observation that can be done in a park, around your neighbourhood, by a window, or on a short walk.

If you want to work week by week, this is the best place to begin.

It is also worth opening the [52-week structure](/en/materials/urban-bathing/52-week-structure.html) to see how the cards form a larger cycle of seasons, themes, and transitions. It helps show that week 1, week 15, and week 40 are all parts of one annual path rather than separate exercises.

If you want a broader introduction, begin with [Urban Forest Bathing: How to Practice Shinrin-yoku Without Leaving the City](/blog/2026/04/03/Urban-forest-bathing-how-to-practice-Shinrin-yoku-without-leaving-the-city/). If you want something ready to use right away, [Free Nature Mindfulness Cards to Download](/en/free-nature-mindfulness-cards-download/) is a strong next step.

## The Page-a-Day calendar as a daily entry point

The [Page-a-Day calendar](/en/page-a-day.html) works as a daily entry point into the same system.

It shows the current day, Polish name day tradition, week of the year, sunrise and sunset, moon details, and the current weekly card. That makes it easy to see where you are in the year and which weekly card is active now.

It is a good choice if you want one simple page to return to in the morning. If you want to understand it better, read [How to Use the Page-a-Day Calendar and What Each Part Means](/en/how-to-use-the-page-a-day-calendar/).

## Sky Today as the lightest form of observation

[Sky Today - the view from your window](/en/sky.html) is the lightest possible entry. It turns your attention toward light, the colour of the sky, the time of day, the length of shadows, sunrise, and sunset.

It is especially helpful when you want to look up for a moment and notice what is already happening around you. Together with the Page-a-Day calendar, it creates a simple daily pair: one page gives the day a shape, the other helps you notice it.

## The blog as an entry point shaped by need

The blog works well when you are looking for an answer to a specific question or a gentle text to begin with.

Three strong starting points are:

- [Mindfulness in Nature: Where to Begin If You Don’t Meditate](/en/mindfulness-in-nature-where-to-begin-if-you-dont-meditate/) if you want a gentle introduction,
- [5 Simple Mindfulness Exercises Inspired by Nature](/en/5-simple-mindfulness-exercises-inspired-by-nature/) if you want practice right away,
- [How to Feel Closer to Nature in the City: 7 Simple Ways](/en/how-to-feel-closer-to-nature-in-the-city-7-simple-ways/) if daily contact with nature in the city matters most to you.

If you want a quieter research backdrop, you can also add [Does Contact with Nature Really Help? What Research Says](/en/does-contact-with-nature-really-help-what-research-says/). A natural next step after reading is to move into one weekly card in [Urban Forest Bathing](/en/materials/urban-bathing/).

## Where to begin

At the beginning, one decision is enough:

- open the [Page-a-Day calendar](/en/page-a-day.html) if you want a daily point of return,
- open the [current weekly card](/en/materials/urban-bathing/) if you want a weekly rhythm,
- or begin with [Mindfulness in Nature: Where to Begin If You Don’t Meditate](/en/mindfulness-in-nature-where-to-begin-if-you-dont-meditate/) if you prefer a calm written introduction.

If you want the wider view later, then visit the [52-week structure](/en/materials/urban-bathing/52-week-structure.html) and see how individual materials form a larger whole.
