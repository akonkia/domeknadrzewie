## The Taste of Memory

Taste can open a memory before we think to look for it. The first bite of an apple may bring back a kitchen table, the scent of a garden, or the presence of someone we have not thought about for a long time. Sometimes the body recognises what the mind was not trying to recall.

This card from the [Urban Forest Bathing](/en/materials/urban-bathing/) series invites you into a brief autumn practice of remembering. All you need is a seasonal fruit and a moment in which you can take the first bite without rushing.

## Mindfulness exercise: remembering

**Theme:** Remembering

Choose a seasonal fruit and take the first bite slowly. Notice the flavour, scent, texture, and then notice what it brings with it. A kitchen, a garden, a person, a place, a particular autumn. Don’t try to search for a memory. Let the taste find one for you.

**Reflection:**

- What memory came back with this taste?
- What part of myself did it bring closer?

## Let the senses lead

Before you take a bite, hold the fruit for a moment. Notice its weight, temperature, and the smoothness or unevenness of its skin. Look at its colours and breathe in its scent. Then take a small bite and allow the flavour to unfold without naming it immediately.

A clear image may arise, there may be only a faint feeling, or nothing particular may happen. Every response is welcome. This practice is not a test of memory. It is an opportunity to notice how a sensory experience can connect the present with something already lived.

## A memory does not need to be a complete story

Sometimes a particular place or person returns. At other times, taste brings only an atmosphere: safety, longing, delight, or the quiet image of an autumn afternoon. You do not need to understand immediately why this is what appeared.

You can stay with one detail — the colour of a tablecloth, the sound of a conversation, or the light in a window. A fragment is enough. Mindfulness allows you to receive it without forcing it into a complete story.

## A small practice for this week

Choose an apple, pear, plum, or another seasonal fruit and give a few minutes to the first bite alone.

Try to:

- feel the fruit resting in your hand,
- notice its scent before tasting it,
- bite slowly and recognise its texture,
- allow the first association to appear,
- write one sentence about what returned.

There is no need to judge the memory or decide what it means. Simply notice where the taste carried you for a moment.

## Week 39 card: The Taste of Memory

This card may feel especially supportive as the days grow shorter and autumn flavours return to kitchens and city markets.

Let the first bite remind you that memory does not live in words alone. Sometimes it finds us through flavour, scent, and the simple gesture of holding fruit in our hands.
