## The Ripe Season

Late summer has the flavour of fruit that no longer needs to wait. Peaches soften, plums deepen in colour, and raspberries and blackberries readily offer their scent. Each one is a reminder that ripening is a process: it needs light, water, time, and care.

This card from the [Urban Forest Bathing](/en/materials/urban-bathing/) series invites you to meet abundance through one simple, everyday gesture. There is no need to travel far. A seasonal fruit or vegetable from a market, shop, garden, or kitchen table and a few moments of genuine attention are enough.

## Mindfulness exercise: abundance

**Theme:** Abundance

Choose something seasonal to eat. Notice its colour, scent, and flavour. Take a moment to enjoy what this part of the year offers. Let it remind you that some things need time before they are ready.

**Reflection:**

What is ready to be enjoyed in my life?<br>
What has ripened through time and care?

## Let the senses slow down

Before you begin eating, look closely at what you have chosen. Notice the shade of its skin, its shape, weight, and scent. As you take the first bite, notice where sweetness, tartness, softness, or crispness appears.

Mindful eating does not require a long ritual. It simply asks you not to move through this moment automatically. Flavour can become a way of meeting the season exactly where you are.

## What is ready deserves attention too

It is easy to focus only on what still needs to be improved, finished, or hurried along. Yet abundance also begins with noticing what has already ripened: a relationship, a skill, a decision, a period of rest, or the small result of daily care.

Ripe fruit does not appear all at once. It is the result of many days that we cannot always distinguish from one another. Life can be similar. Some things become ready quietly. Sometimes we only need to pause long enough to recognise their flavour.

## A small practice for this week

Once a day, choose one seasonal food and give its first minute your full attention, without a phone, conversation, or rush.

Try noticing:

- three colours or shades,
- its scent before the first bite,
- how the flavour changes as you eat,
- its texture and temperature,
- gratitude for the time and work that allowed this food to ripen.

At the end, ask yourself: what in my life is already ready for me to stop only working on it and allow myself to truly enjoy it?

## Week 33 card: The Ripe Season

This card may be supportive when it feels easier to see what is missing than what is already present and valuable.

Abundance does not always mean more. Sometimes it means meeting more fully what has just ripened.
