## The Turning Leaf

A season rarely changes in a single clear gesture. First there is a yellow edge, a warm shade between green veins, or one orange leaf among many summer ones. Only later do we realise that the landscape has truly begun to look different.

This card from the [Urban Forest Bathing](/en/materials/urban-bathing/) series invites you to notice that kind of beginning. The image shows a branch where green meets yellow and orange, with the city still close by. It is a reminder that subtle change does not require a distant forest. You can meet it beside a pavement, in a courtyard, in a park, or outside your window.

## Mindfulness exercise: subtle change

**Theme:** Subtle Change

Find one leaf beginning to change colour. Look closely at where the green gives way to yellow, red, or brown. Notice how the change does not happen all at once: one part shifts while another remains the same. Stay with it for a moment. Let this small turning remind you that change can begin quietly, almost before we notice it.

**Reflection:**  
What small change is beginning in me?  
What do I want to carry with me as the season turns?

## Notice the place of transition

This practice is not about finding the most beautiful autumn leaf. The more interesting leaf is the one that is not yet entirely new or entirely as it was before. In this in-between place, you can see that change has stages.

Look closely at the boundary between colours. Is it sharp or blurred? Does the change begin at an edge, in the centre, or as a single small patch? This kind of attention lets you stay with a process without rushing it or needing to name its ending.

## Small beginnings matter too

Changes within us also often become visible only with time. A new need, a gentler response, a little courage, or a wish for a different rhythm may initially seem too small to count. Yet a new direction often begins with precisely these quiet shifts.

The turning leaf does not leave everything behind at once. For a while, it holds several colours and several stages within itself. It can remind us that we do not need to arrive immediately on the other side of change. We can notice its beginning and allow it to develop at its own pace.

## A small practice for this week

On your daily walk, choose one tree or shrub and return to it over several days.

Try noticing:

- which leaf begins to change first,
- exactly where the new colour appears,
- what still remains green,
- how light affects the shades you can see,
- whether each day reveals something that was not visible before.

At the end, stay with one question: what small change is present in me now, even if I cannot yet know where it is leading?

## Week 32 card: The Turning Leaf

This card may be especially supportive during transitions, when one part of life is already changing while another remains familiar.

There is no need to hurry or complete the process. It is enough to notice the first new colour and give it a little attention.
